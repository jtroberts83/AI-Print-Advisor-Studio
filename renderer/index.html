Tips

  • Download the 3MF file when there is one: it keeps the colors, the parts and the plate. Look for a print profile made for the Snapmaker U1, or any 3MF (the app converts Bambu Lab files).
  • Only an STL? Open it in Snapmaker Orca, then save the project (File, Save Project) as a 3MF and open that here.
  • Check the license. Most models are for personal use only: don’t sell the prints unless the license allows it.
  • Look at people’s photos of their prints (“makes”, comments and ratings): a model lots of people printed well is a safe first pick.

Keep the booklet next to your printer

The AI 3D Print Advisor booklet (7 pages, US Letter) has the steps, the buttons, why a print can still fail, a troubleshooting table, and every 3D printing word with its picture. It’s the handiest thing to have beside your printer while you learn.

Open it and print it from your PDF viewer (Ctrl+P), or save a copy. There’s also a 1-page quick sheet. You can find both anytime in the Glossary window and Settings, and in the “Printable cheat sheets” folder next to the program.

The AI that reviews your models

Snapmaker Orca (the slicer)

The app slices with Snapmaker Orca, free from Snapmaker, to measure your models and compare versions. It wasn’t found on this PC. Download it, install it, then pick snapmaker-orca.exe.

Your filament library

This is where you keep every spool you own: photos, colors, where it’s stored, how much is left and the print settings. Color remix picks its colors from here.

The app comes with a sample library (a real collection of about 240 spools, with photos) so you can see what it does. Explore it, then remove it in Settings (Filament library) when you’re ready to add your own. Anything you add yourself is kept when you remove the samples.

Export for Bambu Lab printers

Makes a Bambu Studio project of this model for sharing on MakerWorld. It uses Bambu's own printer, quality and filament presets, keeps your tuned choices (walls, infill, supports, layer height, colors, orientation), and Bambu Studio itself writes the final file, so MakerWorld accepts it.

Snapmaker Orca color mixes become a filament of their own in the blended color, so the model looks the same. (Blending them on a Bambu AMS would swap spools on every layer: days longer and kilograms of purge.)

Not test-printed on that printer? Say so in your MakerWorld description (e.g. “converted from a tested Snapmaker U1 profile”).
